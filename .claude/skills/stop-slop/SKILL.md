# Stop Slop

Eliminate predictable AI writing patterns from prose.

## Core Rules

1. **Cut filler phrases.** Remove throat-clearing openers, emphasis crutches, and all adverbs. See [references/phrases.md](references/phrases.md).

2. **Break formulaic structures.** Avoid binary contrasts, negative listings, dramatic fragmentation, rhetorical setups, false agency. See [references/structures.md](references/structures.md).

3. **Use active voice.** No passive constructions. **CHANNEL OVERRIDE:** the upstream rule demands a *human* subject in every sentence. This channel does the opposite on purpose - the animal is the protagonist and people only watch from outside. Keep the active voice, let the creature be the actor.

4. **Be specific.** No vague declaratives ("The reasons are structural"). Name the specific thing. No lazy extremes ("every," "always," "never") doing vague work.

5. ~~Put the reader in the room.~~ **CUT.** The narrator is a field biologist watching from a hide, speaking as "I" and never addressing the viewer as "you". Keep only the half that survives: specifics beat abstractions.

6. **Vary rhythm.** Mix sentence lengths. Two items beat three. End paragraphs differently. No em dashes.

7. **Trust readers.** State facts directly. Skip softening, justification, hand-holding.

8. ~~Cut quotables.~~ **CUT.** A nature documentary lives on its closing line. Keep one per episode, at the end of a beat, and earn it.

## Quick Checks

Before delivering prose:

- Any adverbs? Kill them.
- Any passive voice? Find the actor (often the creature) and make it the subject.
- Inanimate thing doing a human verb ("the decision emerges")? Name the actor.
- Sentence starts with a Wh- word? Restructure it.
- Any "here's what/this/that" throat-clearing? Cut to the point.
- Any "not X, it's Y" contrasts? State Y directly.
- Three consecutive sentences match length? Break one.
- Em-dash anywhere? Remove it.
- Vague declarative ("The implications are significant")? Name the specific implication.
- Meta-joiners ("The rest of this essay...")? Delete. Let the essay move.

## Scoring

Rate 1-10 on each dimension:

| Dimension | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Directness | Statements or announcements? |
| Rhythm | Varied or metronomic? |
| Trust | Respects reader intelligence? |
| Authenticity | Sounds human? |
| Density | Anything cuttable? |

Below 35/50: revise.

## CHANNEL OVERRIDES

Three upstream rules are cut or inverted above because they fight this channel's voice. Recorded here
so nobody silently restores them:

| Upstream rule | What we do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Every sentence needs a human subject" | Let the creature be the subject | The channel's whole premise is the animal as protagonist, humans only observing |
| "Put the reader in the room, 'You' beats 'People'" | Cut. Narrator says "I", never "you" | Second person breaks the documentary frame |
| "Cut quotables" | Keep one closing line per episode | Nature documentaries live on the closing line |

Everything else applies as written, and applies *better* than it did upstream: this skill was written
for English prose, and English is now the channel's source language.
