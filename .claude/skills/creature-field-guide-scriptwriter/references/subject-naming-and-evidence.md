# Subject naming and evidence

## Naming hierarchy & Canon mandate
 
1. **No personal names for animals.** This is a hard channel rule (docs/CAST.md).
   - The central individual is **a canon trait + a field code**: "Shiny Bulbasaur · K-01". Prefer the
     Shiny trait when the species has one. Invented names churned four times on episode 001 and each
     round rippled through script, cast sheet and rules.
   - Every other animal is called by its **species**: "a Fearow", "the old female Venusaur". A minor
     animal that recurs and could be confused with others of its species gets a **field code**
     ("K-04"), still no name.
   - **The trait is only spoken after the audience has seen it.** "Shiny" is said only after the shot
     that shows the unusual colour, so that trait needs its own clear shot.
   - "Shiny" is framed as **the locals' word** for such a colour morph, said once, then the code is used.
   - Species label: "Bulbasaur" (never translated). Field codes are identical in both languages.
2. **Strict Canon-Backed Rule (No Unverified Fanon):**
   - Every location name, creature behavior, and individual moniker **must have an explicit reason** rooted in morphology/behavior AND a **verifiable Canon source** (Game Pokédex, Pokémon Anime episode, or Manga chapter).
   - Example: Locations must use official Kanto geography (e.g., **Viridian Forest** where wild Bulbasaur naturally spawn in *Let's Go* canon, or **The Mysterious Garden** from Anime Ep 51) rather than fabricated fanon names.
3. Why codes suit the narrator: naming is a kind of ownership; a field code is observation. Dr. Holth
   observes and never intervenes (docs/NARRATOR.md).

## Evidence labels

Every important claim should be internally tagged as one of:

- `record`: supplied by the creature's field guide or Pokedex-like record.
- `observation`: directly visible in the episode.
- `comparison`: an Earth biology comparison.
- `hypothesis`: the narrator's interpretation.
- `unknown`: intentionally unresolved.

In the spoken script, make the distinction audible with phrases such as “the record says”, “in the field I saw”, “on Earth we know”, “one possibility is”, and “we still do not know”.

## Composite footage rule

If several generated images represent the same subject, keep the subject's visual anchors fixed. If the shot is a reconstruction rather than the same individual, label it as a reconstruction in the production notes and do not use a personal name for it.
