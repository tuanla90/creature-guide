# Episode approval checklist

Use this checklist before recording voice, generating the final image set, or promoting an episode to production.

## Story pressure

- [ ] The episode has one clearly stated survival question.
- [ ] The environment creates a real pressure or deadline.
- [ ] The subject needs, wants, or must achieve something specific.
- [ ] The central conflict is ecological rather than a forced good-versus-evil plot.
- [ ] The three-part arc is visible: setup, confrontation, resolution.
- [ ] The ending either answers the opening question or deliberately refines it.

## Speculative biology

- [ ] Each major ability has a proposed structure, trigger, output, cost, and failure mode.
- [ ] The mechanism is labeled as record, observation, comparison, hypothesis, or unknown.
- [ ] The Earth analogy clarifies the idea without pretending to prove canon biology.
- [ ] The episode shows a consequence of using the ability.
- [ ] The food web identifies resources, predators, competitors, and symbiotic/parasitic relationships where relevant.
- [ ] The transformation mechanism is species-specific; no generic shedding or pain is added without evidence.

## World immersion

- [ ] The narrator has a credible in-world role and source of knowledge.
- [ ] The voiceover does not mention game, CGI, prompt, generation, camera crew, or production tools.
- [ ] Deadpan sincerity is maintained; the creature is not mocked for being comic or weak.
- [ ] Any scientific-style Latin name is explicitly a proposed taxonomy, not presented as canon.

## Subject and naming

- [ ] One central individual has a stable field identifier.
- [ ] The character has at least one visible identifying marker.
- [ ] Any narrative name has a credible in-world source.
- [ ] A composite reconstruction is labeled as reconstruction and is not given a misleading personal name.
- [ ] The chosen archetype is shown through behavior, not asserted by narration.

## Evidence and accuracy

- [ ] Every major claim is tagged as record, observation, comparison, hypothesis, or unknown.
- [ ] Canon facts are not presented as real-world biology.
- [ ] Earth comparisons clarify the fictional creature instead of replacing it.
- [ ] Speculation is signaled with language such as “one possibility”, “I suspect”, or “we cannot yet know”.
- [ ] Transformation mechanics do not introduce unsupported anatomy or biology.

## Narration and sound

- [ ] Sentences are complete and connected; the script does not read like captions.
- [ ] Sentence length varies with the emotional state of the scene.
- [ ] There are planned pauses for natural sound and music.
- [ ] Danger and pursuit use tighter cadence; reflection uses longer phrasing.
- [ ] No paragraph merely repeats what the image already shows.
- [ ] VBee pronunciation and breath breaks have been reviewed aloud.

## Visual continuity

- [ ] Character identity, body proportions, markings, and scale are locked.
- [ ] Environment landmarks persist across shots.
- [ ] Lighting and weather changes have a story reason.
- [ ] Behavior shown in the image matches the narration.
- [ ] Every generated shot has a purpose in the beat sheet.

## Final tone

- [ ] The predator or competitor is not framed as morally evil without evidence.
- [ ] The episode contains curiosity, not only exposition.
- [ ] Emotion comes from stakes and observed behavior, not manipulative adjectives.
- [ ] The final line opens a credible question or bridge to the next creature.
